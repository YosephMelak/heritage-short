## 1. Heritage Research Report — Harar Jugol & Hyena Coexistence

### 1.1 Historical & Architectural Context of Harar Jugol
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site (2006):** Located in eastern Ethiopia, **Harar Jugol** is a fortified historic town dating back before the 13th century and shaped by a 16th-century Islamic town plan, enclosed by the 4–5 meter coral-and-mud ***Jugol* wall** (~3.5–4.5 km long) with five historic gates (*Assaim Bari*, *Erer Bari*, *Buda Bari*, *Sanga Bari*, *Suqutat Bari*, plus the 1889 sixth *Harar Gate*).
- **Urban & Craft Fabric:** Known for narrow winding alleys, 82 mosques, 102 shrines, traditional Harari houses (*Ge Gar*), vibrant straw basketry, and traditional embroidered attire.

### 1.2 Documented Coexistence vs. Oral Tradition
- **Human–Wildlife Symbiosis (Documented):** Wild spotted hyenas (*waraba*) have inhabited Harar's periphery for centuries, patrolling alleys at night to clear organic waste while largely avoiding aggression toward residents. By day Harar is a human space; after sunset, temporal boundaries allow peaceful nocturnal sharing.
- **The Stick-Feeding Practice (Documented Modern History, ~1960s–present):** Regular feeding with meat scraps on short wooden sticks began outside the walls in the 1960s to keep hyenas from attacking livestock and evolved into an intergenerational custodianship.
- **Oral Folklore (Clearly Qualified as Folklore, Not Literal History):** Oral legends connect peace with hyenas to a 19th-century famine pact and an annual *Ashura* butter-porridge (*qoore*) feeding (*waraba nasib*). Harari folklore also views *waraba* ("newsman") as spiritual boundary-keepers.

### 1.3 Visual Heritage Elements Decision Table

| Heritage Element | Description | Cultural Context | Visual Opportunity | Decision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Harar Jugol coral-stone walls & gate** | Weathered arched gate & stone walls outside old town | 16th-century fortified UNESCO town | Warm lantern light + cool moonlight contrast | **Include prominently** (Shots 1, 2, 3, 4, 8) |
| **Traditional Harari attire, lantern & woven basket** | Embroidered tunic, wrapped cotton headcloth, oil lantern, straw basket | Respectful Harari cultural grounding & continuity | Locks consistent visual silhouette (`Umar` left, `Dawit` right) | **Include prominently** (Shots 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 8) |

| **Ge Gar interior / Ashura porridge** | Decorative townhouse interior / annual *qoore* festival | Rich Harari domestic & seasonal traditions | Would fragment the single nocturnal wall setting | **Omit from this 68s nocturnal film** |

### 1.4 Cultural Accuracy Risks to Avoid
- **Consent & Real Families:** Do not use the real Harar family name (`Yusuf` / `Abbas Yusuf`) without consent; use strictly fictional elder keeper **Umar** and young man **Dawit**.
- **No Horror Tropes:** Portray hyenas as calm nocturnal neighbors, not monsters.
- **Subtitle Readability:** Keep English subtitles concise (`<= 2` lines, `<= 40` chars/line) for vertical `9:16` safe areas.

### 1.5 Verified Sources
1. **UNESCO World Heritage Centre — *Harar Jugol, the Fortified Historic Town*** (https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1189/) — Documents 2006 UNESCO inscription, *Jugol* wall, five historic gates, urban layout, and living traditions.
2. **Marcus Baynes-Rock**, *Among the Bone Eaters: Encounters with Hyenas in Harar* (Penn State University Press; reported via *The Guardian*) — Documents ethnographic research on Harar's urban hyenas, temporal/spatial coexistence, *waraba* folklore, and *Ashura* porridge traditions.
3. **Wikipedia / Cultural Heritage Online — *Harar Jugol* & *Hyena Men of Harar*** — Cross-checked timeline context (16th-century *Jugol* wall, 1960s emergence of regular stick feeding, *waraba nasib* idiom).

## 2. Story Concept, Central Message, Blocking & Character Arc

- **Title:** *Keeper of the Hyenas: A Night in Harar — የጅቦቹ ጠባቂ፡ አንድ ምሽት በሐረር*
- **Central Message:** *When humanity and nature meet in respect rather than fear, even the darkness proclaims peace.*
- **Locked Spatial Blocking:** Elder **Umar** on the **left**, young **Dawit** on the **right**, glowing lantern and woven basket between them—culminating in **Shot 8 mirroring Shot 1's wide composition** in calm camaraderie.
- **Music Direction:** Unified `72 BPM` `D minor` instrumental score (`ACE-Step 1.5`, deep resonant *krar* bass plucks, subtle Harari percussive pulse, haunting *washint* flute notes; no vocals): *Tension -> Grounded reverence -> Silence -> Courage -> Peace*.

### Dawit's Character Arc (68s)
1. **Tension (`Shot 1`):** Outside Harar Jugol's coral-stone walls at dusk, Dawit stands tense on the right as glowing eyes appear in the brush.
2. **Observation (`Shots 2–4`):** Elder Umar prepares meat on a short wooden stick beside a straw basket, calls the hyenas softly, and feeds the first calmly—proving they act like neighbors rather than beasts.
3. **Turning Point (`Shot 5`):** Dawit releases his fear, steps forward, and accepts the wooden feeding stick.
4. **Wordless Approval (`Shot 6`):** Silent `5.5s` beat—Umar gives a slow, proud nod in warm lantern light.
5. **Transformation (`Shot 7`):** Dawit kneels calmly in lantern glow and feeds a spotted hyena peacefully under Umar's steady presence.
6. **Mirrored Resolution (`Shot 8`):** Wide frame mirroring Shot 1—Umar (left) and Dawit (right) standing together in peace as the hyenas walk back into the moonlit night.
